Level 1 — understand the basic CLOVER decision

Same farm. Same crop demand. Same weather. One difference: CLOVER can see the short-term forecast.

Start with Steve's Centre Pivot setup. The four equal-quarter IMUs have separate soil-water states, but they share one irrigation system. The pump/system services one eligible IMU at a time in a simple rotating sequence. Baseline irrigates when the SWD rule is met; CLOVER checks the rainfall forecast first and can wait.

SWD says irrigation is due→Forecast checked→Shared pump chooses next eligible IMU→Irrigate now or wait

Level 5 — full CLOVER farm decision

When several IMUs need irrigation and one pump cannot service them all, which IMU goes first?

This level keeps Steve's 11-IMU Mackay traveller geometry and shared irrigation capacity. Baseline uses the simple rotating sequence used in Levels 1–4. CLOVER first decides whether irrigation should proceed or wait for forecast rain, then the selected priority rule chooses among eligible IMUs.

The fixed rotating sequence is the simple baseline rule. Crop stage, highest SWD and highest crop stress are our alternative simulation rules; Steve's workbook defines the farm/IMUs but does not prescribe these priority algorithms.

CLOVER decision workflow

Follow the irrigation decision from current SWD to the final forecast-informed schedule

Only one step is shown at a time. The 45-day synthetic model still runs underneath, but the workflow focuses on the next 14 days so the logic is easier to follow.

1

What is the current SWD across all IMUs?

Start from each IMU's crop stage and synthetic starting soil-water deficit before any future irrigation decision is made.

2

What irrigation settings are we using?

These are the rules that determine when an IMU needs water and how much one shared pump/system can apply.

3

Which IMUs would require irrigation over the next 14 days without using the forecast?

This is the raw irrigation requirement before the shared-pump constraint is applied. More than one IMU can therefore appear on the same day.

4

Which days have an irrigation conflict?

A conflict occurs when two or more IMUs require the one shared irrigation system on the same day.

5

How do we shift irrigation to remove those conflicts?

For the simple baseline, the shared pump follows the fixed rotating IMU sequence. One IMU irrigates; the others wait for the next available slot.

6

What is the final irrigation schedule without forecast information?

This is the feasible Baseline schedule after shared-pump conflicts have been resolved.

7

What does the rainfall forecast look like over the coming 14 days?

Now add forecast rainfall amount and probability. At this step the forecast is information only; we have not changed irrigation yet.

8

What forecast rule should CLOVER use?

A forecast qualifies only when both the rainfall amount and probability thresholds are met within the look-ahead window.

9

How does the forecast change the irrigation requirement?

IMUs that would otherwise irrigate can now be delayed when qualifying rainfall is forecast, unless the soil is too dry to safely wait.

10

What conflict days remain after forecast-based delays?

Forecasting can remove some conflicts, create different ones, or leave the same competition for the shared pump.

11

How do we shift irrigation to remove the remaining conflicts?

Levels 1–4 use the same fixed rotating sequence as Baseline. Level 5 can instead apply the selected priority rule.

12

What is the final irrigation schedule with forecasting?

This is the feasible CLOVER schedule after forecast delays and shared-pump conflicts are both resolved.

13

How do Baseline and CLOVER compare for every IMU and the whole farm?

Inspect each IMU's SWD and irrigation history, then review the farm-level comparison and detailed delay / pump-wait events.
