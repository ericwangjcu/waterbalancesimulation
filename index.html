How the demonstration works

The baseline and CLOVER simulations use the same site, crop demand, weather, irrigation system and water availability. The only difference is that CLOVER can use the short-term rainfall forecast before an irrigation decision.

1
Track crop water deficitDaily crop water use increases soil water deficit (SWD). Rain and irrigation reduce it.
2
Irrigation becomes dueWhen SWD reaches the selected trigger, the baseline irrigates using the selected CP or LM assumptions.
3
CLOVER checks the forecastIf forecast rain meets both the rainfall amount and probability thresholds within the look-ahead period, irrigation can be delayed.
4
Compare the outcomesRealised rainfall is then applied to both simulations and the model compares effective rainfall, SWD, irrigation use and the crop response.
Limited water / drought: better timing can capture more useful rainfall, preserve scarce irrigation for later crop demand and reduce water stress, potentially protecting TCH/TSH.
Non-limited benchmark: better timing can replace some irrigation with effective rainfall, reducing applied water and pumping while maintaining yield.

Steve's initial modelling structure

What is held constant

Site, crop water demand, precision irrigation, scheduling, automation, weather and available irrigation water.

What changes

Baseline has no short-term forecast. CLOVER uses forecast rain amount × probability to modify irrigation timing.

Primary value under limited water

More effective rain can support greater crop water use and reduce water stress, potentially increasing TCH/TSH.

Primary value when water is non-limiting

More effective rain can lower irrigation demand and therefore reduce water and pumping cost while maintaining yield.
